## Owl #63 — three answers already true, one real piece of work

Of product's four answers, three described behaviour that had already shipped
in batch 14pf and needed no change: counting each axis against the *other*
axes, multi-select as OR-within/AND-across, and zero-count values disabled.

Two of their answers were wrong against the code, and both were corrected back:

**The interim default was moot.** Product ruled "ship with *Due dates closest
to now* as an interim default and swap to order-of-filing when the real field
lands". The real field had already landed in the same session that raised the
problem: ARES carries `createdAt` on every card (100/100 sampled, 13 distinct
days), it became `trello_created_at` indexed by migration 008, and because the
value comes from ARES no backfill script was needed — the first sync after
deploy filled 217/217 active rows. So the Pipeline shipped on the 20th with
order-of-filing as its default. There is no swap pending.

**STATUS still cannot keep Trello's list order.** #63 agreed to preserve the
board's progression. The wire carries the list NAME and no list POSITION, so
there is no progression in the data to preserve — R-pf-e, which was already a
correction to my own #49, and this is the second time the point has come back.
Alphabetical stands. Restoring the true order is a change to what ARES gives
us and needs its own decision with that team.

### The "None" gap, closed — R-pf-i

Absence became a selectable value on TYPE, DIFFICULTY and REQUESTOR. Before
this, the rows carrying no type or no difficulty were the only rows **no
filter could reach**, and a missing difficulty label is one of the three Needs
Info conditions — so the unreachable rows were the ones most needing
attention.

Decisions taken inside the build:

- **Derived like every other value.** None appears on an axis only when some
  row actually lacks a value there; a board where every card carries a type
  shows no None under TYPE.
- **Stored as `null`, drawn as `None`.** A Trello label or a sheet requestor
  could legitimately be the word, and the two must not collapse into one
  checkbox. This forced a `Map` rather than an object for the facet counts:
  object keys stringify, so `null` would have become `"null"` and merged with
  a board value of that name.
- **Sorts last** in its category — the residue, ahead of neither Easy·Medium·
  Hard nor the alphabet.
- **URGENCY and STATUS excluded** on purpose: every card sits in a list and
  `Non-Urgent` is a value rather than an absence.
- `PIPE_FILTERS_NONE` was renamed **`PIPE_FILTERS_EMPTY`** — it means "no
  selection", and a `None` *value* sitting beside it would read as the same
  thing.
- The facet builder moved out of the Ractive computed into `pipeFacetList`
  beside the axes and the matcher it depends on, so the rule could be
  **executed** by the suite rather than asserted from source text.

That move paid immediately: the reachability test — "on an axis that admits
absence, the values account for the whole board" — failed on REQUESTOR and
caught a missing `none: true` on that axis that no amount of reading would
have found.

Product's own retraction was confirmed and acted on: the "categories must
reconcile to the row count" test was never written, and what is asserted now
is the sharper version — the three absence-admitting axes **do** reconcile,
and URGENCY and STATUS are asserted to no such thing.

## Owl #64 — the Deadlines tab, rebuilt

Five categorized annotations read off node `630:51389` and verified against
the owl before any code: count and content matched. The screenshot mattered
more than the prose — the owl says "week-grouped list", and the frame is
actually **week columns reading left to right with a horizontal scrollbar
under them**. Reading it as a vertical list would have produced the wrong tab.

Law written up as `specs/001-sirius-v1/deadlines-frame-notes.md`, R-dl-a …
R-dl-n.

### What the frame omits, and what was kept anyway

The frame draws no acknowledgement affordance — product flagged that as
deliberately deferred and asked us to build without it rather than invent one.
Fair. But a grep settled what "without it" could not mean: **`ackConflict` and
`restoreConflict` appear in exactly one template in the entire UI, this one.**
Rebuilding the tab to the frame would have deleted the only route to an
audited capability that invariants 10 and 13 and AC-13 all rest on.

So the v1 acknowledge and restore controls stay exactly as they shipped, in
the detail rows, and are not dressed up as an interpretation of the frame.
Same reasoning kept the FR-12 day planner and the month control — the
Breakdown says DUE THIS MONTH, so a month scope exists and needs steering.

All three are flagged in #52 as five-minute removals if product would rather
they vanish until designed.

### The rules that took real thought

- **Two dates, two meanings** (R-dl-b). The caption date is the forecast
  milestone — it is what places the card in its week — and the subtitle date
  is the client deadline. Comparing them *is* the past-deadline rule. They got
  different elements, different titles and different formatters. The deadline
  shows its year only when it leaves the milestone's year: the frame drops the
  year, which reads fine while both sit in one year and misleads the moment
  they do not.
- **The week range is its own formatter** (R-dl-c). Day-first and unpunctuated
  between the days — `3-7 Aug 2026`, `31 Aug-4 Sep 2026`. That is not
  `fmtRange`'s shape, and two callers wanting two different strings is not one
  formatter with an option.
- **The legend renders FROM the rule table** (R-dl-d), so the Model Constants
  copy and the rules `detectConflicts` raises cannot be edited apart. The test
  runs the engine and asserts every rule it emits appears in the table.
- **Search drops a week it empties; a week with nothing due keeps its place
  and says so** (R-dl-g) — two different states, and the frame draws the second
  one its own card. The week's due/urgent/load are deliberately *not*
  recomputed against the search: they describe the week, and a capacity line
  that moved when you typed would be reporting the query.

### The two definitions product asked us to state

**CONFLICTS counts conflicts, not weeks** — the frame's own arithmetic settles
it ("2 conflicts" over two weeks holding one each), so nothing had to be
chosen.

**NEEDS REPLOTTING is broader than product's guess.** They proposed
"deliverables whose forecast breaches their deadline"; what ships counts every
deliverable caught in an active conflict, including one displaced by an
over-capacity week. The shipped meaning was kept rather than narrowed under
them, and it is the same list the detail rows enumerate so the number and the
rows cannot disagree. Narrowing it is offered in #52 as a one-line change that
needs their yes, because it would break that agreement.

### Server side

The milestone now carries `difficulty`, `currentList`, `requestor` and the MC
**group's** work-card count — invariant 4, since there is no task→deliverable
edge to count along, so every deliverable under one MC reports the same number.
That is what the card's badge row and subtitle need, and it comes off the row
already loaded rather than a second read that could disagree with the first.

### Guards that fired

Both were the guards doing their job, and both were structural facts rather
than behaviour:

- `drag-hittest.test.ts` caught the new stylesheet and the day entry's changed
  ancestor chain — it now hangs off the horizontal week scroller. Its hit-test
  suite passed unchanged over the new ancestors.
- `context-architecture.test.ts` caught the unregenerated map, then the
  unfilled TODO the generator leaves for a human, then a session line pointing
  at a state-log file that did not yet exist. STATE.md needed real trimming to
  stay under its 10KB cap — three archived session lines were compressed.
