### The "None" gap, closed — R-pf-i

Absence became a selectable value on TYPE, DIFFICULTY and REQUESTOR. Before
this, the rows carrying no type or no difficulty were the only rows **no
filter could reach**, and a missing difficulty label is one of the three Needs
Info conditions — so the unreachable rows were the ones most needing
attention.

Decisions taken inside the build:

- **Derived like every other value.** None appears on an axis only when some
  row actually lacks a value there; a board where every card carries a type
  shows no None under TYPE.
- **Stored as `null`, drawn as `None`.** A Trello label or a sheet requestor
  could legitimately be the word, and the two must not collapse into one
  checkbox. This forced a `Map` rather than an object for the facet counts:
  object keys stringify, so `null` would have become `"null"` and merged with
  a board value of that name.
- **Sorts last** in its category — the residue, ahead of neither Easy·Medium·
  Hard nor the alphabet.
- **URGENCY and STATUS excluded** on purpose: every card sits in a list and
  `Non-Urgent` is a value rather than an absence.
- `PIPE_FILTERS_NONE` was renamed **`PIPE_FILTERS_EMPTY`** — it means "no
  selection", and a `None` *value* sitting beside it would read as the same
  thing.
- The facet builder moved out of the Ractive computed into `pipeFacetList`
  beside the axes and the matcher it depends on, so the rule could be
  **executed** by the suite rather than asserted from source text.

That move paid immediately: the reachability test — "on an axis that admits
absence, the values account for the whole board" — failed on REQUESTOR and
caught a missing `none: true` on that axis that no amount of reading would
have found.

Product's own retraction was confirmed and acted on: the "categories must
reconcile to the row count" test was never written, and what is asserted now
is the sharper version — the three absence-admitting axes **do** reconcile,
and URGENCY and STATUS are asserted to no such thing.

### The rules that took real thought

- **Two dates, two meanings** (R-dl-b). The caption date is the forecast
  milestone — it is what places the card in its week — and the subtitle date
  is the client deadline. Comparing them *is* the past-deadline rule. They got
  different elements, different titles and different formatters. The deadline
  shows its year only when it leaves the milestone's year: the frame drops the
  year, which reads fine while both sit in one year and misleads the moment
  they do not.
- **The week range is its own formatter** (R-dl-c). Day-first and unpunctuated
  between the days — `3-7 Aug 2026`, `31 Aug-4 Sep 2026`. That is not
  `fmtRange`'s shape, and two callers wanting two different strings is not one
  formatter with an option.
- **The legend renders FROM the rule table** (R-dl-d), so the Model Constants
  copy and the rules `detectConflicts` raises cannot be edited apart. The test
  runs the engine and asserts every rule it emits appears in the table.
- **Search drops a week it empties; a week with nothing due keeps its place
  and says so** (R-dl-g) — two different states, and the frame draws the second
  one its own card. The week's due/urgent/load are deliberately *not*
  recomputed against the search: they describe the week, and a capacity line
  that moved when you typed would be reporting the query.

### The two definitions product asked us to state

**CONFLICTS counts conflicts, not weeks** — the frame's own arithmetic settles
it ("2 conflicts" over two weeks holding one each), so nothing had to be
chosen.

**NEEDS REPLOTTING is broader than product's guess.** They proposed
"deliverables whose forecast breaches their deadline"; what ships counts every
deliverable caught in an active conflict, including one displaced by an
over-capacity week. The shipped meaning was kept rather than narrowed under
them, and it is the same list the detail rows enumerate so the number and the
rows cannot disagree. Narrowing it is offered in #52 as a one-line change that
needs their yes, because it would break that agreement.

edge to count along, so every deliverable under one MC reports the same number.
That is what the card's badge row and subtitle need, and it comes off the row
already loaded rather than a second read that could disagree with the first.

## Rex changed what was findable

The panels were measured through **Rex against the component's own auto-layout**,
not read off screenshots. That is the whole reason this session found what it
found — a screenshot shows a state, the component shows the STATES:

- the selected sort was rendering as **bold text**; the item's `State` variant
  `Selected` is a filled slate-900 pill with a white label and the weight left
  at Regular. Bold is the obvious way to mark a choice and it is not what the
  frame does.
- a checked filter box **filled but showed no tick**; the Checkbox component
  does both. Without the tick the two states differ only by colour, which reads
  as a swatch rather than a checkbox.
- a checked filter value was **bold**; checked and unchecked labels are
  byte-for-byte identical in the frame — the selection lives entirely in the
  box. The sort panel and the filter panel express selection in OPPOSITE ways
  on purpose, which is exactly the thing that would have been "made consistent"
  by anyone working from pictures.
- **Clear** had one colour for two states; the Button-Small carries a `Color`
  variant, blue when there is something to clear and slate when there is not.

⚠️ Practical note for next time: `mcp__rex__get_status` returns the channel.
STATE.md had 7782 written down and the live server was on 7780, which cost JP a

## Where we deliberately left the frame — seven places, all recorded

Every one is in R-pf-j/k/l/m with its reason, because each reverses something
the frame states and would otherwise read as drift:

1. **Both panels anchor to the toolbar container, not to their own button.** The
   frame anchors each panel under its trigger — exactly, to the pixel, once the
   64px coordinate-space offset is corrected. But the sort button GROWS to 240px
   when it names its selection and drags the filter button 196px sideways. A
   static frame cannot show movement. JP saw it live and ruled.
2. **Zero-count values are hidden, not disabled** — the annotation says the
   opposite in words, and on the real board it filled STATUS with rows nobody
   could pick. Exception preserved: a value already TICKED that falls to zero
   stays, or the reader is stranded with a filter they cannot see or un-tick.
3. **The row's sides**, moved twice in one day: symmetric 16/16 first, then 16
   right / 8 left so the CHECKBOX lines up with the group heading. Both cannot
   hold; JP chose alignment after seeing each.
4. **The heading's sides are 16, not 24**, for the same reason.
5. **A chip's hover panel insets 16 where the full panel insets 20** — one group
   and no footer, so the room five groups need reads as slack.
6. **The 160px label cap is gone from filter values**, kept for sort where it
   drives the two-line rows.
7. **The panels wear the card-issue popover's shadow**, not the frame's lighter

## The Filter Indicator

One chip per filtered AXIS — `Type is Icon, Asset` — with a ✕ that clears the
axis and a blue Clear all. ⚠️ The component's `Number` variant counts the values
INSIDE a chip, not the chips; the `2` variant is a single chip listing two
values under one axis name and one ✕. Read the other way it becomes a different
feature, and it would have looked plausible.

Two rules the frame could not state, because it only ever draws one chip: the

Hovering a chip opens that chip's own group — the frame's cut-down panel, footer
and scrollbar switched off — and JP ruled it **clickable**, which is what shaped

## ⚠️ The lesson worth carrying: two cascade traps in one file in one day

A single-class override loses to a shared rule declared later in the same file.
It bit twice:

- `.chipmenu { right: auto }` lost to `.pipemenu { right: 0 }`, and the panel
  only landed near the right place because a box with left, right AND width is
  over-constrained so the browser dropped `right`. It would have snapped to the
  far edge the day the width changed.
- `.chipmenu .pmitems { padding-bottom: 0 }` lost to `.pipemenu .pmitems`, so
  the list kept its 8px under a comment of mine claiming it was zeroed.

**Both times the CSS read correctly and the browser disagreed.** Neither was
caught by a test; both were caught by measuring the live page. Overrides on
`.pipemenu` now go on `.fchip .pipemenu` or `.sortfilter .pipemenu` — two
classes cannot lose whatever the order — and the anchoring itself moved onto the
container that owns it, so there is less to override at all.

# Session 4 — the Forecast tab (`66549c6`, `9a0f817`)

- Four Forecast frames exist in Figma, and no `forecast-frame-notes.md` did.

The tell that settles it: `frontend/styles/` had `20-pipeline`, `25-requests`,

## The tab with no annotations

This is the only tab in the project with **no owl and no Figma annotations**. The
`annotations` property is absent on every node in all three frames,
`get_design_context` returns no annotation block, the Rex note store is empty, and

Every other tab was built from an owl that stated intent and a frame that showed

## ⚠️ The frame quotes the retired formula

The footer banner's first Model Constant reads **`Total Cycle Time · 1.28 ×
Forecasted Review Time + 2.96`**.

Those are `LEGACY_CYCLE` — the retired workbook regression. `lib/forecast.legacy.ts`
implements it, `test/forecast.legacy.test.ts` asserts it **against `legacyForecast`
only**, invariant 6 confines it to migration tests, and **SC-3 says in the spec's
own words that the spreadsheet formula "is never exposed"** because it overstates

It is also not a description of the shipped engine, which computes
`lead + design + review` twice over.

Building the panel verbatim would have printed the exact constants the constitution
retired, on the one screen whose entire purpose is that the retired model is gone —
under a heading that calls them the model's constants. The panel renders the real
relation instead, and the other three constants (Friday of the sketch-approval week ·
WORKDAY with PH holidays excluded · WEEKNUM of the start date) are quoted as drawn,

It is almost certainly inherited from the prototype's own Model constants panel,
which displayed exactly those two numbers back when they were the model. Raised so
it cannot be copied forward again.

## Three typos, evidenced before they were corrected

The rule in this project is that the frame wins. It is a rule about design intent,
not about demonstrable errors, and this frame carries three:

- **The second breakdown group is labelled `SKETCH`**, in both target frames,
  confirmed three ways (Rex `characters`, `get_design_context`, the pixel render).
  It holds the render figures. Three independent sources say so: §7.2's own list,
  the fields it renders, and the frame's own sample numbers, which differ between

- **`CYLCE`, three headers** — while the same frame's footer spells *Total Cycle

- **The confidence select is headed `TYPE`** — a word that already means asset type

  wire field called `confidence`. Ships as **CONFIDENCE**.

All three raised to product rather than changed under them, all one line to revert.

## ⚠️ THE CASCADE TRAP, A THIRD TIME — and the first one a test catches

`.fctable .fcslagroup { background: var(--status-warning-light) }` was written to
tint the Review SLA group. **It never rendered.** The band rule above it is
`.fctable .fctier1 th` — two classes *and a type* — which out-specifies two classes
wherever either sits in the file. The amber-800 label text won (only `.ptable th`
contested it), so the cell drew dark-orange lettering on the same grey as every
other group: a stray coloured word instead of a tinted block.

The first two instances of this class, both on 2026-08-21, were caught **only by
measuring a live page**. This one is caught by a guard that **computes
specificity**: for every rule in the sheet that paints a header cell, it finds the
tier band that rule is trying to beat and fails a tie.

It found a second instance the moment it ran (`.fcspacer`, same shape), plus one
accident — the muted-figure block was body-only in intent and matching header cells
too, now scoped to `td`. And `.fctable .fc-total`'s emphasis had no `td` in it, so
the dark ink was landing on that column's tier-two header as well: one header cell
reading slate-900 beside twenty-four reading slate-500.

**A tie is not a win, and a reader's eye reports it as one.** That is the sentence
worth carrying — the previous two write-ups said "declared later loses", which is
true but incomplete, and the incompleteness is what let it happen a third time.

## ⚠️ The SLA field cannot be corrected through the model

`{{#each forecastRows as row}}` iterates a **computed**, so `forecastRows.N.slaSketch`
is a read-only computation child. Ractive's two-way binding never attaches — it
logs `Cannot use two-way binding … is read-only` — and **the typing never reaches
the model at all**.

The reject branch therefore wrote `prev` back to a keypath that already held `prev`.
Ractive drops that on an equality check, nothing re-renders, and the refused number
sat on screen under a banner saying it had been kept. A `badInput` field is worse:
it reports its own value as the empty string, so **no model write of any kind can
reach it**.

The branch now sets the control itself. That is the only thing that puts the last
committed value back and the only thing that makes the banner true.

This one was found by writing the guard's own claim down — R-fc-t said the field
"re-renders with the good number" and nothing tested it. The review's correctness
lens bound the real template with the real library and watched the warning appear.

## The older defect this build fixes

`setSla` and `setConfidence` had **no failure path at all**. The server refuses a
review SLA outside 0–60 with a 400, `api.send` throws, neither handler caught it,
and there is no `unhandledrejection` handler anywhere in the app. A refused number
stayed on screen indefinitely with no banner, no revert and no reload — while the
four neighbouring writes (`writeDeadline`, `writeTaskDue`, `writeCapacity`,
`writeDayPlan`) have always done optimistic-with-revert plus `flashBanner`.

§7.3 also requires non-numeric input to be *"rejected without clearing the field"*,
while a `type="number"` input reports unparseable text as the empty string — which
the old code read as a deliberate **clear** and used to delete the override.

## What the review found

Five lenses, every finding attacked by an independent skeptic; nine survived of
seventeen. Three were code defects (above). The other six were guards that a
mutation proved empty:

- the twenty-five hand-typed body cells were unguarded on alignment and on which
  field each one reads — only the header tiers fold from `FC_COLS`
- the fallback span was **recomputed by the harness** instead of executed, so both
  the computed and its binding could be replaced by a wrong literal
- the SLA bound was asserted to exist *somewhere in the bundle* rather than to be
  spent by the handler
- the scroller resolver was two independent substring matches, so the new `.fcwrap`
  branch could have been wired to the wrong thumb key
- nothing at all executed the code that picks between the two empty states

Six mutations are caught now where five were before, and the sixth is the cascade
class itself.

## Kept, against a spec section that only describes a table

Three things the old view carried are not in §7 and stay: the green
model-provenance panel (**FR-7.7 / AC-11**, and the only place in the product where
BR-3's overstatement is stated to a user), the per-row `n` (**AC-11 requires
"provenance and sample size visible" in those words**, so it is a 25th column no
frame draws), and the late-render red treatment (**AC-18**, demoed live on real
data). Deleting any of them to match a spec section about column layout would have
dropped an acceptance criterion.

## One thing that looks like a bug and is not

The render breakdown is **numerically identical to the sketch breakdown**, today,
by construction: `renderDesign` and `sketchDesign` are both `l`, the reviews are
both `o`, and the cycles differ only by a lead constant that is 0.5 on both sides.
Eight columns where four are duplicates reads as a defect. It is not — §7.1's whole
point is parity with the Delivery Forecast sheet's own columns, so the team reads it
unchanged. Each block renders its **own** four fields and never copies the other's,
so the day the engine distinguishes them the table shows it without an edit.

## Owed

**The whole tab has never been deployed and never been seen in a browser.** The
eight `it.todo` in `test/forecast-frame.test.ts` are the list: whether the two
header tiers stay in column alignment through a horizontal scroll, whether 2704px
scrolls inside `.pscroll` and never the page body, whether the sticky header sticks
or is inert (`.pscroll` has no height, so `top: 0` may pin to a box that never
moves), whether the Forecast and Pipeline sliders fight after a tab switch, and
whether a rejected SLA visibly keeps its old number.

Given this project's own lesson — the filter panels were inert on the live site for
a day under a green suite — none of that is settled until a pointer touches it.
