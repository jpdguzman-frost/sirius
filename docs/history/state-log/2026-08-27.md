## Questions answered back, from the code rather than by asking

- **#69, the field key.** No divergence to record — it was already `requestor`
  end to end. `intake-parser.ts:54-55` maps both `requestor` and
  `primary requestor`; the API never returned `client`. The rename was
  display-only.
- **#70/#72/#73/#74, can the forecast come from ARES.** No, not per row.
  `computeDeliveryForecast()` (`ares/src/services/demandService.js:300`) is a
  Monte-Carlo simulation over daily throughput vs total demand cards — it
  answers "when does this queue of N finish" and converts with
  `addWorkingDays(today, days)`. Finest granularity is `perLane`. Its axis is
  **lane/discipline × throughput**, never difficulty. `computeCardCycleTime()`
  is per-card but backward-looking: measured dwell on cards that already
  moved. So the existing split is the right one — ARES supplies the
  measurements, Sirius derives the per-item date. The two quantities are not
  interchangeable.
- **#72/#74, does anything still consume review time.** Yes, and in a place
  nobody would look: `late` was `renderDelivery > deadline`, and
  `renderDelivery` counts from the Friday of the sketch-approval week. The
  client's wait was inside **every** past-deadline warning in the product.
  Defensible while the schedule drew a review segment and auto-placed render
  behind it; not defensible once #71 removed the bar and #72 made render a
  hand-placed row — review then drove a warning while being visible nowhere
  and adjustable nowhere, the one control that could move it having left with
  the Forecast tab. → ruling 3.
